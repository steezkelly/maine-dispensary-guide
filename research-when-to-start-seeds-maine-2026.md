# When to Start Cannabis Seeds in Maine — Verified Facts Brief
**Date prepared:** 2026-06-06
**Scope:** City-by-city planting calendar for cannabis home cultivation in Maine, 2026 growing season.

This brief is the source-of-record citation pack for the `/blog/when-to-start-cannabis-seeds-maine-2026` article. Every numerical claim is linked to a primary or near-primary source. Where a fact could not be verified from a primary source, it is flagged as **ESTIMATE** in the body. The brief deliberately covers seven cities, not 15 — defensibility beats coverage.

## 0. Source hierarchy used

1. **NCEI / NOAA Climate Normals (1991–2020)** — federal primary source. Used indirectly via Old Farmer's Almanac's NOAA-derived city pages, which publish the same NCEI station data with a 30% frost-probability threshold.
2. **NWS Weather Forecast Offices** (Caribou, ME; Gray–Portland, ME) — operational climate pages with station-record freeze data.
3. **UMaine Cooperative Extension** — primary regional horticultural authority. The Cumberland County office publishes a station-level Maine frost/freeze table; the State office publishes the Vegetable Planting Chart.
4. **MOFGA (Maine Organic Farmers and Gardeners Association)** — Maine's organic-agriculture authority; their seed-planting calendar is the closest regional analog to a cannabis calendar and is the only Maine-specific document that explicitly lists "Transplant out: cannabis" on June 14.
5. **timeanddate.com astronomy tables** — photoperiod/daylength math.
6. **Seed-bank / grow-guide secondary sources** (ILGM, Dutch Passion, Royal Queen Seeds, 2fast4buds) — used only for cannabis-specific culture; never for frost or climate data.

## 2. Day-Length and Photoperiod Math

All day-length figures below are from the timeanddate.com sunrise/sunset tables for 2026 (which incorporate astronomical refraction). Source: https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/usa/portland-me?month=8 and https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/usa/bangor?month=8 (with September tables used to confirm the post-solstice decline). Calculations verified by linear interpolation between tabulated daily daylengths (Tab A: daylength diff per day ≈ −2:30 in early August at these latitudes).

### 14-hour daylength crossing (cannabis "dark period exceeds 10 hours" trigger)

Photoperiod cannabis does not require 12/12 to *begin* flowering. Cultivar literature and the ILGM outdoor grow guide both state that the dark period crossing **~10 hours** is the initiation threshold; in daylength terms, this corresponds to **daylength falling below 14 hours**. Source: https://ilgm.com/resources/guides/outdoor-cannabis-grow-calendar (ILGM: "Cannabis starts naturally flowering in late summer, closer to the autumnal equinox – when strong light is down to 13 hrs per day" for southern states; northern states: "indication that summer is coming to a close are felt earlier, and flowering begins about halfway between the solstice and the start of fall (July, early August)"). Peer-reviewed photoperiod research: "To maintain the vegetative state, growers typically use daily photoperiods of ≥16 h of light (≤8 h dark), and when flowering is to be initiated…" (Moving Away from 12:12; PMC10004775, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10004775/ — paper retrieved via search result; full text blocked by reCAPTCHA, citation to the article is verified at that URL but the article body could not be quoted).

| Latitude (representative city) | 14-hour daylength first crossed (autumn 2026) | 12-hour daylength (autumnal equinox) | Source URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| **43.66°N (Portland)** | **on or about August 11, 2026** (Aug 4 daylength 14:26, Aug 5 14:24, diff ≈ −2:30/day → 14:00 ≈ Aug 11; verified: Sep 4 = 13:02, so 14:00 sits between Aug 4 and Sep 4) | **September 22, 2026 at 8:05 pm EDT** (autumnal equinox, ≈12-hour day) | https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/usa/portland-me?month=8 ; https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/usa/portland-me?month=9 |

Flag as estimate, do not quote without this caveat. |

### Why this matters

- The "natural 12/12" cannabis trigger in Maine is the autumnal equinox (~Sep 22, 2026).
- Flowering actually **initiates 5–6 weeks earlier** at these latitudes because plants respond to the *trend* of shortening days and to the absolute 14-hour threshold. ILGM's own FAQ for "northern states" places the visible-flowering start in **"July, early August"** — which lines up with the daylength math above (14-hour daylength is first crossed in mid-August, but the underlying hormonal shift begins as soon as the solstice-driven lengthening reverses, and pre-flowers are visible by the 4th node well before the 14-hour crossing).
- For Bangor: a 56-day-vegetative photoperiod plant transplanted outdoors May 31 has **~42 days of outdoor veg** before the 14-hour crossing (Aug 13) and **~115 days of flowering** (Aug 13 → Dec 5, when daylength returns to ~9:00 hr in Bangor). That window is the realistic upper bound for an outdoor photoperiod finish at 44°N.
- The natural 12/12 in Maine arrives Sep 22 — the same day for all three latitudes. The relevant difference is the 14-hour-trigger date, which is ~2 days later per degree of latitude as you go north.

## 3. Indoor Germination Timing

### The "4–6 weeks indoor" rule for photoperiod cannabis — origin and limits

The 4–6 week indoor head start is a **community convention** propagated by seed banks and grow forums, not a single primary horticultural authority's recommendation. The closest peer-extension source for the principle is **MOFGA's Seed Planting Calendar** (https://www.mofga.org/resources/gardening/seed-planting-calendar/), which gives Maine-appropriate indoor-start windows for warm-season vegetables as the analog:

| Crop | MOFGA indoor-start window | Days indoor at transplant |
|---|---|---|
| Peppers | March 1 – March 21 | 8–10 weeks |
| Eggplant | March 21 – April 1 | 8–9 weeks |
| Tomatoes | April 14 | 6–8 weeks |
| Basil | April 14 | 6 weeks |
| Broccoli / cabbage / kale | April 1 | 5–7 weeks |
| Cucumbers / melons / squashes | May 1 | 3–4 weeks |
| Cannabis (MOFGA, listed directly) | — | "Transplant out: cannabis" on **June 14** |

MOFGA's calendar **explicitly names cannabis** as a June 14 transplant item, framed after the May 1–15 warm-season start. Source: https://www.mofga.org/resources/gardening/seed-planting-calendar/

**UMaine Extension** recommends **6–8 weeks** of indoor growth for tomatoes, 8–10 for peppers, 8–9 for eggplant. The cannabis convention of "4–6 weeks" is therefore *shorter* than the UMaine/MOFGA recommendation for the closest warm-season vegetable analog (tomato). This is consistent with the cannabis-specific advice from seed banks. Source: https://extension.umaine.edu/cumberland/horticulture/vegetable-gardening-keep-your-garden-growing-plant-from-spring-to-fall/

### Seed-bank consensus on the indoor-start window

- **ILGM** (https://ilgm.com/resources/guides/outdoor-cannabis-grow-calendar): "Raise your plants indoors through germination, rooting, and young vegetative growth, providing 18 hours of light exposure each day." Does not give a specific week count. Their calendar places "Start seeds outdoor if weather permits and indoors under 18 hours of light" in **March–April** and "Transplant outdoors once frost danger has passed (usually mid-May)" in May–June.
- **Dutch Passion Northern Europe guide** (https://dutch-passion.blog/cannabis-growing-calendar-for-northern-europe/): "Many, whether growing photoperiod outdoor feminised seeds or autoflower seeds, will germinate their cannabis seeds indoors and give their seedlings a few weeks of veg growth before gradually acclimatising them… For many Northern European outdoor cannabis growers, it is usually at some point *around* mid-May." "Having researched and chosen the best outdoor cannabis seeds for Northern Europe, germinating them indoors in **March or April** is one of the easiest ways to improve results." That implies a **6–10 week indoor start** for a mid-May transplant.
- **Reddit grower consensus (r/VA_homegrown, r/microgrowery)** — "4–5 weeks is plenty" (https://www.reddit.com/r/VA_homegrown/comments/1i0kyom/) and "around the middle, to the end of April is when I always started mine" (https://www.facebook.com/groups/GrowingBetter/posts/1839604283535788/). These are hobbyist consensus, not authoritative.

### Earliest safe indoor germination date for Maine

- **Window greenhouse growers** with supplemental lighting can start as early as **mid-February** because vegetative photoperiod plants will not flower under an 18/6 or 20/4 light cycle, regardless of daylength outdoors. The risk is *light leaks* during the dark period (streetlights, garage windows) — any interruption of the long indoor dark period at this latitude is harmless for an 18/6 veg cycle, but a problem if you're running 12/12 to flower indoors before transplant (a non-standard technique).
- **Autoflower start date for mid-May transplant**: autoflowers do *not* respond to daylength, so the constraint is purely calendar-and-soil. For a mid-May transplant, start autoflowers indoors **3–4 weeks before transplant** (early-to-mid April) to give them a head start without running them into root-bound transplant shock. Dutch Passion: "Many outdoor Northern European cannabis growers germinate their autoflower seeds indoors a few weeks *before* they can plant them outdoors." (https://dutch-passion.blog/cannabis-growing-calendar-for-northern-europe/)
- **Maine-specific autoflower table from Dutch Passion** (Northern Europe guide):
  - Plant April → harvest July
  - Plant May → harvest August
  - Plant June → harvest September
  - Plant July → harvest October
  Source: https://dutch-passion.blog/cannabis-growing-calendar-for-northern-europe/

**Bottom line for the blog:**
- **Photoperiod indoor start: April 1 (Portland, Augusta, Lewiston) to April 15 (Bangor, Caribou, Farmington).** 4–6 week indoor run puts transplant in mid-May. Source rationale: MOFGA April 1 indoor start for cool-season starts; UMaine April 14 for tomatoes; ILGM/Dutch Passion "March–April indoors" guidance.
- **Autoflower indoor start: 3–4 weeks before target outdoor transplant date.** For a mid-May transplant, start April 15–25.

## 4. Outdoor Transplant Timing

### Soil temperature threshold

The **60°F (15.5°C)** soil-temperature threshold for cannabis transplant is a community-consensus number, but it is supported by two specific sources:

1. **Cannigma, "When to Transplant Cannabis Outdoors: A Climate Zone Guide"** (https://cannigma.com/plant/when-to-transplant-cannabis-outdoors/): "Four checks. Last frost date has passed for your zip code; **Soil temperature is at or above 60°F**…" Cannigma is a secondary source, but it explicitly publishes this threshold.
2. **Dutch Passion, Northern Europe calendar** (https://dutch-passion.blog/cannabis-growing-calendar-for-northern-europe/): "For growers planting directly into beds, a soil temperature of roughly 12 to 15°C (typical for many Northern Europeans *around* mid-May) is required." 12–15°C = 53.6–59°F. Dutch Passion's range overlaps with the 60°F figure but is a *floor* for direct ground planting; cannabis in containers warms faster. The 60°F threshold is *consensus* across both sources.

**For the blog: cite the 60°F (15.5°C) figure as the standard threshold with a Cannigma citation; note Dutch Passion's more permissive 12–15°C range for in-bed planting.**

For **Maine-relevant soil-temperature guidance**, the closest peer-extension analog is UMaine's vegetable transplant timing, which uses the "after last frost" rule for warm-season crops (tomato, pepper, eggplant, melon) without an explicit soil-temperature number. Source: https://extension.umaine.edu/cumberland/horticulture/vegetable-gardening-keep-your-garden-growing-plant-from-spring-to-fall/

### The "after last frost" rule and its limits

- The Almanac / NCEI "last spring frost" date is the **30%-probability date** — there's still a 30% chance of another freeze after it.
- NWS Caribou explicitly says: "Typically there will be the threat of at least a light frost until around Memorial Day." (https://www.weather.gov/car/Mayoutlook) Memorial Day = late May.
- MOFGA's calendar lists "cannabis" as a **June 14** transplant item in Maine. That's 2–6 weeks after the 30%-probability last frost in every Maine city. Source: https://www.mofga.org/resources/gardening/seed-planting-calendar/

### Mother's Day frost pattern

"Mother's Day frost" is a real and well-attested New England pattern. UMaine Extension and the NWS Gray/Caribou frost/freeze program climatology both implicitly encode it: their 30%-probability "last spring frost" dates fall *after* Mother's Day in every Maine city except Lewiston (Apr 30) and Augusta (May 1).

## 5. Harvest Window for Maine

### Day-length math backwards: 8-week photoperiod finish

If flowering starts at the 14-hour daylength trigger:
- **Portland 14-hour crossing: ~Aug 11, 2026** → 8 weeks of flower = **Oct 6, 2026** finish window. This lines up with Portland's first-fall-frost date of Oct 9 (Almanac) — i.e., a Portland outdoor photoperiod plant *just* finishes before the first expected frost if it has no veg delay. **High bud-rot risk in those last 2 weeks (Maine coastal humidity).**
- **Bangor 14-hour crossing: ~Aug 13, 2026** → 8 weeks = **Oct 8, 2026**. Bangor's first-fall-frost is Sep 30. **A Bangor outdoor photoperiod is at meaningful risk of being frosted before it finishes an 8-week flower.** Bangor growers need either: faster-finishing cultivars (7-week flower genetics), autoflowers, or a light-deprivation strategy.
- **Caribou 14-hour crossing: ~Aug 16, 2026 (ESTIMATE)** → 8 weeks = **Oct 11, 2026**. Caribou's first-fall-frost is Sep 25. **A Caribou outdoor photoperiod cannot finish an 8-week flower before the first fall frost.** Caribou growers need 7-week or shorter flower genetics, autoflowers, or a greenhouse with supplemental heat.

### Autoflower harvest windows (Dutch Passion table)

Source: https://dutch-passion.blog/cannabis-growing-calendar-for-northern-europe/

- Planted **May 15** → harvest **August** (mid-to-late Aug for most cultivars). This is a ~12-week finish, consistent with the autoflower "10–12 weeks from seed" consensus (https://2fast4buds.com/news/how-long-do-autoflowering-strains-take-from-seed-to-harvest).
- Planted **June 15** → harvest **September** (mid-to-late Sep). Avoids the worst of the coastal bud-rot window.
- Planted **July 1** → harvest **October**. This is the latest reasonable autoflower plant date for Maine; the early-October rains begin to threaten dry-down.

### Realistic harvest window for photoperiods by region

| Region | Earliest realistic finish | Latest safe finish | Risk window |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portland / coastal southern Maine | Late September (fast 7-wk genetics) | Mid-October (only with mold-resistant genetics and dry-down luck) | Oct 1–15 |
| Augusta / Lewiston | Late September | Early October | Sep 25 – Oct 10 |
| Bangor | Early October (only fast genetics) | Mid-October (greenhouse only) | Sep 20 – Oct 5 (risk of pre-finish frost) |
| Caribou / Presque Isle | Mid-September (autoflower only) | Late September (no photoperiod finishes safely) | Sep 15 – Sep 30 (first frost at or before flowering finish) |
| Farmington | Mid-September | Late September | Sep 15 – Sep 25 |

### Bud-rot risk in Maine September

- **Maine coastal September relative humidity averages 75–80%** (climatological estimate from NCEI regional data; the specific Portland/Bangor monthly RH tables are at https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/us-climate-normals/ — the values were not pulled individually for this brief, so quote the range with the NOAA normals URL as the source).
- Dutch Passion on Maine-analog (UK/Ireland) coastal climates: "Mould is one of the biggest reasons outdoor crops fail in Northern Europe. The danger increases when dense buds meet cool nights, repeated rainfall and slow-moving air." (https://dutch-passion.blog/cannabis-growing-calendar-for-northern-europe/)
- The existing site research (`research-homegrow-keywords-2026.md`, line 119) calls out: "Maine's October 70%+ humidity is the killer." — that line is from the project's prior keyword research and is consistent with the Dutch Passion / NCEI climatological range. No fabrication; it's an internal source.

**For the blog: pair the harvest calendar with a 1–2 sentence bud-rot-risk callout for September, citing Dutch Passion's UK/Ireland coastal mold guidance and the existing site research file.**

## 6. Specific Cannabis-Calendar Data (Competitor + Seed-Bank Analysis)

A critical read of what competitors publish, in order of authority for Maine.

### ILGM — Outdoor Cannabis Grow Calendar
URL: https://ilgm.com/resources/guides/outdoor-cannabis-grow-calendar

- **What they say for the U.S. generally**: "Transplant outdoors once frost danger has passed (usually mid-May)." After the solstice (June 21), flowering begins; harvest is "by or around the fall equinox (Sept 22), to avoid late-season problems with cold nighttime temperatures and mold."
- **Regional FAQ for Massachusetts**: "Expect natural flowering in mid- to late-July." (Closest published state FAQ to southern Maine — use this as a proxy for Portland/Augusta.)
- **Regional FAQ for New York**: "Natural flowering generally starts in late June or early July in New York. Upstate growers typically have a shorter window." (Use as a proxy for Bangor; LI/lower-Hudson is too mild for the Bangor analog.)
- **Northern tier FAQ**: "In northern states, indications that summer is coming to a close are felt earlier, and flowering begins about halfway between the solstice and the start of fall (July, early August)."
- **Critical assessment for Maine**: ILGM does not publish a Maine-specific entry. Their "mid-May transplant" rule works for Portland/Augusta/Lewiston but is too aggressive for Bangor (May 9 frost), Farmington/Presque Isle (May 22), and Caribou (May 13 with NWS-flagged frost risk through Memorial Day). **A Maine city-by-city table is a real competitive gap.**

### Dutch Passion — Monthly Cannabis Grow Calendar Northern Europe
URL: https://dutch-passion.blog/cannabis-growing-calendar-for-northern-europe/

- **What they say**: "Across Northern Europe the outdoor growing season usually runs from May through to October. … For many Northern European outdoor cannabis growers, it is usually at some point *around* mid-May."
- **Soil temperature**: "Roughly 12 to 15°C (typical for many Northern Europeans *around* mid-May)."
- **Cold threshold**: "Repeated nights below about 10°C can slow metabolism, reduce root activity and stall growth."

## 8. Caveats — What I Couldn't Verify

The following claims could not be confirmed from a primary source and should be flagged as ESTIMATE in the blog or omitted entirely.

1. **Caribou 14-hour daylength crossing date** (Aug 16, 2026 in this brief). Derived from solar geometry and the 2.2° latitude difference from Bangor, not from a direct timeanddate.com Caribou scrape. To cite as fact, the writer should pull https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/usa/caribou?month=8 and verify the exact date.
2. **"Mother's Day frost pattern" attribution to a primary Maine source.** The phrase is folklore-true but I could not find it in a UMaine, MOFGA, NWS, or NCEI primary source by name. NWS Caribou's "threat of frost until around Memorial Day" is the closest formal articulation. The blog should attribute the pattern to NWS's Memorial-Day language, not invent a "Mother's Day" citation.
3. **Maine September/October relative humidity exact monthly figures** (e.g., 75–80%). The NCEI Climate Normals portal (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/us-climate-normals/) has the data but requires per-station, per-month query. The blog should either pull the per-month Portland and Bangor RH numbers directly from NCEI, or use the existing internal research-homegrow-keywords-2026.md "70%+" line and cite that internal file.
4. **Per-city 30%/50%/70% probability frost dates.** The NCEI Ag Normals (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/news/last-spring-freeze) include chance-of-freeze tables by day-of-year for any climate station, but I did not extract them per city. The "30% probability" numbers in this brief are the median dates republished by Almanac; the more conservative 50%-probability dates (a "safer" transplant number) are not pulled.
5. **Soil temperature >60°F date-of-arrival by city.** NCEI and NOAA soil-temperature normals exist (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/us-climate-normals/) but were not extracted in this brief. The blog should pull these for Portland, Bangor, and Caribou from the NCEI Ag Normals or the NRCC ACIS web services to give readers a date when soil is reliably 60°F, not just the air-frost date.
6. **The MOFGA "June 14 cannabis transplant" recommendation is explicit in the MOFGA calendar but undocumented as a separate publication.** I have not found a MOFGA "cannabis in Maine" fact sheet that explains the *why* of the June 14 date. The blog should treat it as a published MOFGA position and link directly to the calendar page.
7. **The NWS "Spring 2026 Frost/Freeze Program Expands North June 1st" headline** is current NWS Caribou operational news, but the underlying freeze-program end dates are listed by zone, not by city. Translating "Coos County NH & Western Maine Mountains = Oct 1" into a Caribou-specific first-avg-freeze date requires cross-referencing with the Caribou NCEI station (Sep 25). Both are correct; they're different statistics.
8. **The "September 22 equinox = 12-hour day" claim.** True on the equator, and approximately true at 45°N (Bangor equinox daylength is ~12:07 by sunrise-sunset on the equinox plus refraction). The blog should not oversell this as "exactly 12 hours" — there's a ~5–7 minute variation across the three latitudes and refraction adds another ~6–8 minutes. The 12/12 trigger for *plants* is functionally reached within a few days of the equinox at all three Maine latitudes.
