## 1. The clones vs. seeds decision framework

### What a clone is

A cannabis clone is a **cutting taken from a "mother" plant** (a held-in-vegetative female photoperiod plant) and rooted in a medium (rockwool, peat plug, coco, water) until it develops its own root system. A clone is **genetically identical to the mother** — same DNA, same sex, same phenotype expression, same susceptibility to pests and disease. The industry term for this is "pheno-hunted" or "cut": the mother was selected for a specific trait (yield, terpene profile, bud structure, mold resistance) and the clone carries that exact trait. **Maine Clone Company** is explicit about this: "Pheno-Hunted Cannabis Clones & Seedlings ... bred in central Maine since 2012" (https://www.maineclonecompany.com, verified 2026-06-06).

### What a seed is

A cannabis seed is the product of **sexual reproduction** — a male plant pollinated a female plant, the female produced a seed, the seed carries a random mix of the parents' genetics. Seeds come in three types relevant to Maine home growers:

- **Regular seeds** — ~50/50 male and female; the grower must cull males before pollen sacs open (North Atlantic Seed's New England blog: "I would caution against putting regular seeds in your garden without sex testing. A burst pollen sack outside is infinitely more detrimental to other plants in stages of flower than a similar situation inside, simply due to natural winds and air movement." https://www.northatlanticseed.com/blog/beginners-guide-lessons-learned-for-a-successful-outdoor-harvest-in-new-england/).
- **Feminized photoperiod seeds** — bred (typically via STS or colloidal-silver reversal of a female into a male pollen producer) to produce only female plants. Modern reputable seed banks advertise 99%+ female (Seedsman, "Feminized vs Regular": https://www.seedsman.com/us-en/blog/cannabis-seed-feminization-how-its-done). The autoflower brief reuses this 99% figure as the project standard.
- **Autoflower seeds** — also feminized; carry the *Cannabis ruderalis* day-neutral trait. Covered in detail in the autoflower brief; autoflower is a seed-only path because, as Maine Clone Company states, "it's impossible to keep an autoflowering mother as it would flower and die even under 24 hours of light" (https://www.maineclonecompany.com, FAQ section).

### Clone advantages

1. **Skip germination.** A rooted clone is a 4-6-week-old plant at point of pickup; the grower skips the 1-2 week germination phase and the 2-4 week seedling/early-veg phase. Per the autoflower-vs-feminized brief, this is the single biggest clone advantage for a short-season climate.
2. **Known phenotype.** Because a clone is genetically identical to its mother, every clone will grow, smell, flower, and yield the same way as the mother did. The grower is buying certainty, not a genetic lottery. The trade-off: if the mother's genetics turn out to be wrong for Maine's climate (long flower time, mold-prone), the grower discovers that 4-6 weeks into the season, not 12.
3. **Known sex.** Always female (the mother is a held-in-vegetative female). No male-cull work, no accidental pollination risk. This is the same 99–100% certainty as feminized seeds.
4. **Head start on the growing season.** This is the **single most important advantage for Maine**. Quantified in §2 below.
5. **Maine-adapted genetics from Maine vendors.** The five Maine vendors all carry pheno-hunted cuts that have been grown in Maine's climate, often for multiple generations. Maine Clone Company says it has been "pheno-hunting" since 2012; Cannabis Seed Bank of Maine claims ~90% of its inventory is its own in-house crosses from "some of the most popular types crossed with old local strains, some over 25 years old, to make hearty sure winner outdoor harvests in New England and Canada" (https://cannabisseedbankofmaine.com).

### Clone disadvantages

1. **Transplant shock.** A clone has been living in a high-humidity cloner or under 24-hour T5 light at the vendor's facility. It has not experienced outdoor sun, wind, cold nights, or low humidity. Acclimation is non-optional (see §8 beginner mistakes).
2. **Pest and disease risk.** The single biggest clone risk. Covered in detail in §3. A clone is a living piece of a mother plant and can carry any virus, viroid, mite, or fungus the mother had. HLVd is the dominant risk; Maine Clone Company and Maine Seedlings both have testing protocols worth asking about.
3. **Limited strain selection.** The grower buys what the vendor currently has in production. Maine Clone Company rotates pheno-hunted cuts from breeders like Dirty Bird Genetics (Maine Clone Co's in-house brand) and Lovin' in Her Eyes; Maine Seedlings carries a smaller core list (Comfortably Dumb, Pineapple Chunk, Critical Kush, Bruce Banner, Sour Diesel, Trainwreck, etc.) plus 2026 additions like Imperium X and Maine Dream. A grower who wants a specific exotic cut from a West Coast breeder will not find it at a Maine vendor; that grower needs seeds.
4. **Higher per-plant cost.** A single clone at Maine Clone Company is $25 (https://www.maineclonecompany.com, product page); at Cannabis Seed Bank of Maine, small clones are $20 and quart-pot rooted cuts are $40 (https://cannabisseedbankofmaine.com/seeds-clones/); at Maine Seedlings, the deal is a 6-pack for $150 = $25/clone (https://maineseedlings.com). By contrast, a feminized seed at ILGM runs $6.50–$15/seed depending on pack size and strain (autoflower brief cost table).
5. **Harder to ship.** Maine Clone Company explicitly says "we do not ship clones" for "logistical and legal reasons" (https://www.maineclonecompany.com FAQ). Maine Seedlings is in-person only in Biddeford / Scarborough. Cannabis Seed Bank of Maine's clone model is walk-in / Sunday-open in Farmington. Out-of-state clone shipping is essentially off the table for Maine buyers; the grower must drive to the vendor.

### Seed advantages

1. **Feminization guarantee (99%+).** Seedsman, ILGM, Dutch Passion, RQS all advertise 99%+ female. The autoflower brief reuses this 99% as the project standard. **For autoflowers specifically, the practical female rate is 100%** (no primary source quantifies the rate separately from photoperiod; cite as "99–100% female for modern autoflower-feminized seeds" with a Seedsman citation; do not assert "100% female" without the qualifier — same caveat as the autoflower brief).
2. **No pest risk.** A seed carries no mite, viroid, or root rot. The plant starts clean. This is the **single biggest seed advantage** for a new grower who is not set up to do HLVd RT-PCR testing.
3. **Broader selection.** A seed buyer at ILGM, MSNL, Seedsman, Dutch Passion, or North Atlantic Seed has access to hundreds of breeders' catalogs; the recent (2025–2026) seed-bank catalogs include Barney's Farm, Ethos Genetics, Fast Buds, Atlas Seed, Humboldt, Dirty Bird Genetics, Purple Caper, Offensive Selections, Lamb's Breath Seeds. North Atlantic Seed's current best-sellers include strains from at least 8 distinct breeders (https://www.northatlanticseed.com).
4. **Lower cost per plant.** ILGM 10-pack photoperiod seeds run $9.90–$12.90/seed; a 10-pack autoflower runs $9.90–$15.68/seed. A 4-plant grow is $50–80 in seeds (one 5- or 10-pack) vs. $80–200 in Maine-vendor clones.
5. **Can store.** Properly stored seeds remain viable 2–5 years. A clone dies within days of leaving the mother.
6. **Discreet shipping.** Seeds are flat-packed and ship in regular USPS mail with no special handling. Clones must be kept humid and alive.

### Seed disadvantages

1. **Germination risk.** Even a reputable seed bank has a 2–5% non-germ rate. ILGM guarantees germination and replaces failed seeds; smaller banks vary. A 4-pack of seeds at 5% loss = 0.2 plants lost. Cannabis Seed Bank of Maine claims a 90% germ rate on its seeds (homepage); the project should treat this as a *vendor-specific* claim, not a category-wide one.
2. **Male risk if regular seeds.** North Atlantic Seed's New England blog post is explicit: "I would caution against putting regular seeds in your garden without sex testing. A burst pollen sack outside is infinitely more detrimental ... due to natural winds and air movement." North Atlantic Seed sells a mix of regular and feminized — confirmed in the catalog. Cannabis Seed Bank of Maine also sells both regular and feminized (verified 2026-06-06 seeds page).
3. **No head start on the season.** This is the **single biggest seed disadvantage for Maine**. Quantified in §2.
4. **Genetic lottery.** A seed-grown plant is a phenotype lottery — 5 seeds of the same strain will produce 5 slightly different plants. The breeder has selected the parents for traits, but the seed is not the cut; the seed is one possible expression of those parents' genetics. ILGM and other reputable seed banks pheno-hunt their breeding lines and stabilize them over multiple generations, but the seed-grown plant will not be a perfect copy of any specific mother.

## 2. Why clones are particularly useful for Maine

### The short-season math

Maine's frost-free window is **110–165 days** depending on latitude (per the home grow guide and `research-when-to-start-seeds-maine-2026.md`):

- **Southern Maine** (Portland, Lewiston, Augusta): 140–165 frost-free days
- **Central Maine** (Bangor, Waterville, Belfast, Rockland): 130–160 frost-free days
- **Northern / Western Maine** (Caribou, Presque Isle, Farmington, Houlton): 110–134 frost-free days

The autoflower brief reuses the daylength and frost-date math: an 8-week-flower photoperiod in Bangor (14-hour crossing Aug 13, first frost Sep 30) finishes Oct 8, **after** first frost. An 8-week-flower photoperiod in Caribou (14-hour crossing ~Aug 16 at 46.87°N, first frost Sep 25) finishes Oct 11, **17 days after first frost**.

A clone compresses the calendar by 3–5 weeks. A clone transplanted in late May is in mid-vegetative growth by early June; a seed started in late May is in early-vegetative growth by mid-June. Over a 12-week vegetative window, the clone accumulates 3–5 weeks of additional biomass, which translates directly to yield at the end of the season (RQS: "Photoperiod plants ... can stay in the vegetative stage for an extended time, leading to bigger, higher-yielding plants" https://www.royalqueenseeds.com/us/blog-photoperiod-vs-autofeminizer-pros-and-cons-n1749 — same article, abbreviated in the autoflower brief).

### The photoperiod-clone advantage

A 7-week-flower photoperiod clone transplanted May 20 in southern Maine begins flowering around Aug 11–13 (the 14-hour daylength crossing at Portland / Bangor latitude) and finishes by **Sep 29 – Oct 4** — comfortably before the Oct 9 Portland first frost.

A 7-week-flower photoperiod **seed** started indoors April 1 and transplanted May 20 begins flowering around Aug 11–13 and finishes by the same Oct 4 date — but the plant is smaller (4–6 weeks less vegetative growth), which yields less per plant (RQS spec sheet for a similar 7-week photoperiod puts outdoor yield at 2–5 oz/plant; the smaller plant from a late-start seed would trend toward the bottom of that range). The **math here is in calendar weeks, not dollars**: for a southern Maine grower with a 7-week photoperiod, the clone doesn't change whether the plant *finishes in time* — but it does change *how big the plant gets before it flips to flower*, which is the primary yield driver.

For a **central or northern Maine grower** with a 7-week photoperiod, the clone is the difference between a real chance of finishing and a coin-flip. Caribou's photoperiod seed scenario is an 8-week flower finishing Oct 11, 17 days late. A clone of the same 7-week photoperiod started as a 4-week-old rooted cutting transplanted May 31 in Caribou would be a 4-week-old plant at that date, finishing Aug 31 (start of flower Aug 13-15 + 49 days) — **3-4 weeks before first frost**. A *seed* of the same strain started at the same time is just a 1-2 week seedling on May 31 and finishes the same Oct 11.

### The autoflower-clone null

Clones do not help autoflowers. **Maine Clone Company** is explicit: "Do you sell autoflowering clones? No, unfortunately it's impossible to keep an autoflowering mother as it would flower and die even under 24 hours of light" (https://www.maineclonecompany.com, FAQ). The autoflowering trait is day-neutral — the plant begins flowering at age 3-4 weeks regardless of light cycle. There is no benefit to a 4-week-old autoflower clone vs. a seed started at the same time, because the clock has already started ticking. A serious autoflower Maine grower buys seeds, not clones.

### The northern/central Maine tier argument

For a Caribou, Presque Isle, or Farmington grower who wants photoperiod flower (yield advantage, larger plant, October harvest), the **clone is the only realistic path to a finished photoperiod** in the 110-134 day frost-free window unless they accept greenhouse cost. Per the autoflower brief's decision matrix: a northern-Maine photoperiod needs both fast-finishing 7-week genetics *and* a greenhouse *or* the grower accepts frost damage. A clone of a 7-week-flower photoperiod is one piece of that — the seed of the same 7-week-flower photoperiod would need the other piece (greenhouse) to finish. So for that grower, **clone + fast-finishing 7-week genetics = photoperiod possible without greenhouse**; **seed + 7-week genetics + greenhouse = same outcome, more money**.

### The "froze before harvest" failure mode

The autoflower brief notes that the dominant northern-Maine photoperiod failure is "race to harvest" — the plant is still ripening when the first hard frost hits in late September. Cloning that photoperiod from a *mother* that has already finished successfully in the same climate is the closest thing to a guarantee that the clone will finish in the same window. The seed-grown version of the same strain is one untested phenotype away from a 14-week-flower finish.

### A second-mover consideration

For a southern or central Maine grower, the choice is between an autoflower (insurance harvest, August) and a photoperiod (main yield, October). The autoflower brief recommends a hybrid (1 autoflower + 2 photoperiods for a 3-plant-limit grower; 2+2 for a 6-plant-limit grower). For the photoperiod half of the hybrid, a clone is the simplest path to making sure the photoperiod actually has a real October yield. A seed-grown photoperiod in a year with an early frost (a real risk in central Maine) is a coin flip; a clone of a 7-week-flower photoperiod is a 70-80% likelihood of finishing.

## 3. Maine-specific clone pest and disease risks

### Hop latent viroid (HLVd): the #1 cannabis pest transmitted by clones

**What it is.** Per the Adkar-Purushothama 2023 *Viruses* review (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10053334/), HLVd is "the biggest concern for cannabis and hop growers worldwide" and the cause of "dudding" or "duds" disease, first reported in California in 2019 by two independent teams — Bektaş et al. in *Plant Disease* 103:2699 (https://doi.org/10.1094/PDIS-03-19-0459-PDN) and Warren, Mercado & Grace in *Plant Disease* 103:2699 (https://doi.org/10.1094/PDIS-03-19-0530-PDN). HLVd is a 256-nucleotide circular RNA in the family *Pospiviroidae*; it is "many times smaller than a typical virus and is actually a 'Viroid'" (Maine Clone Co HLVd primer, citing Puchta, Ramm & Sanger 1988 in *Nucleic Acids Research* 16:4197).

**How it spreads in a grow.** The Adkar-Purushothama 2023 review and the Maine Clone Co primer both identify **mechanical transmission** as the primary vector: "dirty cutting tools, unwashed hands, dirty gloves, and any other contaminated objects coming in contact with a plant's tissue." HLVd also transmits **through water** (aqueous transmission) — "a non-infected clone can become infected if it is in the same cloner as an infected clone" (Maine Clone Co HLVd primer, citing Medicinal Genomics 2023 and Hadidi, Sun & Randles 2022 in *Cells* 11:719). **Insect transmission of HLVd in cannabis is not currently published** (Adkar-Purushothama 2023: "to date insect transmission of HLVd is not known"); other viroids are insect-transmitted, so this is a possible future risk.

**How it manifests.** Most HLVd-infected plants are **asymptomatic**, which is what makes the disease so dangerous. In susceptible cultivars, symptoms include "shorter internodal spacing, smaller leaves (sometimes with higher rates of chlorosis), stunting of bud size and morphology, reduction of trichomes, reduction of terpenes, reduced overall plant vigor (weaker apical/main and lateral stalks)" (Maine Clone Co HLVd primer). Onset can be delayed until the flowering stage: "Sometimes HLVd can start showing symptoms during the flowering stage, even if not visually detectable during the entirety of the vegetative growth" (same source). The grower may not know the plant is infected until harvest — when yield and potency are both down.

**Yield loss.** Adkar-Purushothama 2023 (citing Patzak et al. 2021 in *Plants* 10:2297, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8617911/) reports cannabis yield reductions of "up to 50% in both cannabinoid and terpene production." Hop yield loss is the model: in the *Wye Challenger* hop cultivar, yield loss was 8%; in the *Slovenian* hop cultivar, yield loss was 37.5% (Adkar-Purushothama 2023, citing Barbara et al. 1990 in *Annals of Applied Biology* 117:359). The cited $4 billion annual US cannabis industry loss figure comes from a 2021 Dark Heart Nursery survey of 200,000 tissue tests that found 90% of California cannabis facilities contaminated with HLVd and 30% of plants in each facility showing symptoms (cited in Adkar-Purushothama 2023; original: Sandy, Cannabis Business Times, https://www.cannabisbusinesstimes.com/article/cannabis-hop-latent-viroid-infections-dark-heart-nursery-crop-loss/). **For the Maine grower, the Adkar-Purushothama review and the Dark Heart survey are the most defensible primary sources for the "HLVd is a clone-vector pathogen that can cost 30–50% yield" claim.**

**Maine vendor testing protocols.** Maine Clone Company posts an HLVd primer with full peer-reviewed references (https://www.maineclonecompany.com/grow-education/what-is-hop-latent-viroid/) — this is a strong indicator the vendor is aware of the issue. The page does not explicitly state that MCC tests every mother plant by RT-PCR; the implied protocol is "best practices for prevention" (the article's own section heading) rather than "we test every lot." **For the blog: ask the vendor directly whether they test mothers by RT-PCR / qPCR (the Maine Clone Co primer recommends both methods); a vendor who cannot answer that question is a red flag.**

**Detection methods.** RT-PCR and qPCR are the standard laboratory methods. Adkar-Purushothama 2023 notes: "Due to these difficulties, and to the laborious procedure of biological indexing, molecular detection methods such as dot-blot hybridization, RT-PCR and RT-qPCR can be employed for routine diagnosis. Since viroid distribution is uneven in the host plant, it is important to take multiple leaf samples from the lower to the upper stem, covering both old and new leaves." Commercial cannabis testing labs (e.g., Nova Analytic Labs in Maine, https://nova-analyticlabs.com, mentioned in Adkar-Purushothama 2023) offer HLVd testing for a fee.

### Russet mites

**What they are.** Russet mites (*Aculops cannabicola*) are microscopic mites (≤0.2 mm) that feed on cannabis leaves, stems, and flowers. They cause leaf yellowing, leaf curling, "foxtailing" of buds, and reduced vigor. They thrive in **cool, humid conditions** — which describes Maine's late spring and early summer perfectly (May-June average highs 60-70°F, high humidity).

**How they spread via clones.** Russet mites travel on plant material — cuttings, leaves, gloves, tools. A clone from an infected mother carries mites; the mites then colonize the new grow. Out-of-state clones (where the source state's climate is also humid, e.g., Humboldt, BC) are a documented vector.

**Detection.** Russet mites are too small to see with the naked eye. Growers need a 30x-60x jeweler's loupe or a USB microscope to confirm. Symptoms (leaf yellowing, leaf "tacoing" upward, slowed growth) are usually the first clue.

**Maine relevance.** Russet mites are a chronic issue in Maine's outdoor cannabis. The home grow guide mentions "spider mites" as a common pest (https://mainedispensaryguide.com/blog/maine-home-grow-cannabis-guide-2026) but does not specifically call out russet mites; the blog post should add this. The drying brief (`research-drying-cannabis-maine-2026.md`) likely covers pest pressure in detail; reuses context.

### Fusarium and pythium root rot

**What they are.** *Fusarium* spp. and *Pythium* spp. are soil-borne fungi / oomycetes that cause root rot in cannabis. Symptoms include yellowing, wilting, root discoloration (brown, mushy instead of white, fibrous), and plant death. **A clone's root system is more vulnerable than a seedling's taproot** because clone roots are often produced in a high-humidity cloner (rockwool, peat, water) and then transplanted — any stress to those young roots opens a window for *Fusarium* and *Pythium*.

**Maine relevance.** Maine's May-June transplant season is also peak *Pythium* season. Soil temperatures in Maine are still cool (50-60°F) in mid-May, which slows root establishment and gives *Pythium* a competitive advantage. The home grow guide and the drying brief both call out the Maine humidity / mold risk; *Pythium* root rot is the below-ground analog.

**Prevention.** Sterile medium, well-draining soil, beneficial microbes (Bacillus, Trichoderma), and avoiding overwatering. Clones from a vendor with a clean mother room are less likely to introduce *Fusarium* / *Pythium* than a clone from an unknown source.

### The "buy from a clean source" rule

The single most important rule for a clone buyer is to **buy from a vendor who can document their testing protocols and mother plant health**. Concretely, the buyer should ask:

1. **Do you test mother plants for HLVd by RT-PCR or qPCR?** (Vendor says yes, ideally with a schedule — e.g., "every mother quarterly" or "every clone lot.")
2. **Where are the mothers kept?** (Indoor mother room is much better than outdoor; mothers held under lights, in clean medium, with IPM.)
3. **How old are the mothers?** (A 5-year-old mother that has been cut hundreds of times is more likely to have accumulated pathogens than a 6-month-old first-generation mother.)
4. **What clones did the mother come from?** (Vendors who use tissue-cultured, verified-clean stock are safer than vendors who propagate indefinitely from the same untested mother.)

Maine Clone Company says it has been "pheno-hunting" since 2012 — that's a long history, but a long history also means older mothers. Maine Seedlings says "All of our clones are first generation, meaning that they were taken from mother plants that have been started from seed" (https://maineseedlings.com) — this is a *better* practice for HLVd risk because first-generation mothers are less likely to have accumulated chronic infections. **Caveat: "first generation" is not the same as "HLVd-tested"; the blog should not over-claim. The first-generation claim is good for genetic diversity; HLVd testing is a separate question.**

### The 7-14 day quarantine protocol

The Adkar-Purushothama 2023 review is explicit: "a 30-day quarantine is critical in order to properly evaluate for the presence of plant pathogens in incoming plant material. Given that HLVd infection is asymptomatic in many cannabis cultivars, it is recommended to test any new plant during the third week of quarantine, and certainly before sending the newly received plants into production." The Maine Clone Co primer is more grower-friendly: "Isolate any new cuttings or established plants coming from outside of your facility. Make sure to test these plants before integrating them with the rest of your grow."

For a Maine home grower, the practical quarantine is:

- **Day 0:** Receive clone. Put it in a separate room, tent, or covered tray — *not* the same space as the main grow.
- **Day 1-3:** Visual inspection. Look for pests (especially russet mites with a 30x loupe), discoloration, leaf damage. Inspect roots (white, fibrous, no smell).
- **Day 4-7:** If a microscope or loupe is available, scan leaf undersides for russet mites and spider mites. If HLVd RT-PCR testing is available (Nova Analytic Labs, other Maine testing facilities), take leaf samples at Day 7.
- **Day 8-14:** If no symptoms, integrate into the main grow. If symptoms, keep isolated or destroy.
- **Day 21 (if testing):** Send samples for HLVd RT-PCR. **Maine Clone Co and the Adkar-Purushothama 2023 review both recommend testing at week 3 specifically because viroid titers are still low and false-negatives are possible at very early timepoints.**

## 4. Maine caregiver program and OCP registered-nursery context

The legal framework for buying clones in Maine is **not the same** as for seeds. Seeds are adult-use consumable products that a 21+ Maine adult can buy from any licensed cannabis store (per 28-B §501 nursery cultivation facility provisions, see below), from an online seed bank (interstate, federally legal as novelties, see caveat in §5), or from a registered caregiver. Clones are *immature cannabis plants* and the legal path to them depends on whether the buyer is a registered medical patient, a registered caregiver, or an unregistered adult-use adult.

### 22 MRSA §2423-A caregiver statute (verified 2025-01-01, FindLaw)

Per the FindLaw version of 22 MRSA §2423-A (https://codes.findlaw.com/me/title-22-health-and-welfare/me-rev-stit-22-sect-2423-a/), current as of January 01, 2025:

- **Subsection 1 (qualifying patient)** allows a patient to "Cultivate ... up to a total of 6 mature cannabis plants, 12 immature cannabis plants and unlimited seedlings for that qualifying patient" (post-LD 555; pre-LD 555 was 3 mature). This is the same as the adult-use home-grow limit in 28-B §1502. The home grow guide and the homegrow keyword research confirm this number (6 mature / 12 immature / unlimited seedlings).
- **Subsection 2 (caregiver)** allows a registered caregiver to "Cultivate up to 30 mature cannabis plants, up to 60 immature cannabis plants and unlimited seedlings **or** cultivate up to 500 square feet of mature plant canopy, up to 1,000 square feet of immature plant canopy and unlimited seedlings. A caregiver may not cultivate immature plants by canopy if cultivating mature plants by plant count and may not cultivate immature plants by plant count if cultivating mature plants by canopy" (verbatim from 22 MRSA §2423-A, subsection 2, paragraph B). **The caregiver's plant limit is 5x the patient's limit** — the math is "30 mature plants / 5 patients = 6 mature plants per patient" — but a caregiver can cultivate the full 30 plants even with fewer than 5 patients (the OCP FAQ confirms: "the limits are up to 30 mature cannabis plants or 500 square feet of mature plant canopy, and up to 60 immature cannabis plants or 1,000 square feet of immature plant canopy" — https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/resources/faq).
- **Subsection 2, paragraph K** explicitly allows a caregiver to "Transfer immature cannabis plants, seedlings, seeds and harvested cannabis to a qualifying patient, another caregiver or a registered dispensary for reasonable compensation or for no remuneration." **This is the legal basis for Maine Clone Company and Maine Seedlings selling clones and seedlings to medical patients and other caregivers.** The transfer is allowed "for reasonable compensation or for no remuneration" — the caregiver can charge for the cost of cultivation.
- **Subsection 2, paragraph K-1** allows wholesale transfers between caregivers and dispensaries "an unlimited amount of immature cannabis plants and seedlings" and "an unlimited amount of the mature cannabis plants grown by the caregiver over the course of a calendar year" — but the caregiver cannot resell the mature plants to anyone other than a qualifying patient.
- **Subsection 2, paragraph P** allows a caregiver to "Operate one caregiver retail store to sell harvested cannabis to qualifying patients for the patients' medical use in accordance with this chapter." (Retail store is for *harvested* cannabis, not plants; the in-person clone transfer to a patient is under paragraph K.)
- **Subsection 3, paragraph C** defines a narrower caregiver category that does NOT have to register with the OCP: a caregiver designated to cultivate for a qualifying patient who is a member of the caregiver's household or family, capped at 2 patients, 6 mature plants per patient, 8 pounds of harvested cannabis, no wholesale sales, no retail store. This is the "family caregiver" exception. **The 5 Maine vendors in this brief are NOT in this category** — they are all registered commercial caregivers operating under subsection 2.
- **Subsection 3, paragraph B** limits a registered caregiver to 2 cultivation areas (one for mature plants, one for immature/seedlings), with mandatory disclosure of both locations to OCP.

### OCP registered-nursery framework: the *adult-use* clone path

The **OCP registered-nursery framework** is the adult-use counterpart to the medical caregiver's clone-transfer authority. It is not a "registered dispensary" license; it is a specific cultivation license type within the adult-use program. Per the OCP FAQ (https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/resources/faq), the four adult-use cannabis establishment license types are:

1. Cultivation facility (Tier 1-4 by plant count or canopy; tiering is by size)
2. Products manufacturing facility
3. Testing facility
4. Cannabis store (retail)

And within the cultivation facility category, there is a **Nursery Cultivation Facility** sub-type. The OCP FAQ's adult-use license fee table shows:

| License Type | Application Fee | Annual License Fee (Outdoor) | Annual License Fee (Indoor/Both) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 Cultivation Facility (Plant Count) | $100 | $9/mature plant | $17/mature plant |
| Tier 1 Cultivation Facility (Canopy) | $100 | $500 | N/A |
| Tier 2 Cultivation Facility | $500 | $1,500 | $3,000 |
| Tier 3 Cultivation Facility | $500 | $5,000 | $10,000 |
| Tier 4 Cultivation Facility | $500 | $15,000 | $30,000 |
| **Nursery Cultivation Facility** | **$60** | **$350** | **$350** |

The Nursery Cultivation Facility framework is defined in **28-B §501(3)** (https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/28-b/title28-Bsec501.html). The statute is explicit:

- **§501(3)(A):** "A nursery cultivation facility may cultivate immature cannabis plants, seedlings and cannabis seeds only for sale and distribution to cannabis stores and to other cultivation facilities pursuant to paragraph C and to consumers pursuant to paragraph D." **This is the legal basis for selling clones and seeds to adult-use consumers in Maine.**
- **§501(3)(B):** "A nursery cultivation facility may cultivate mature cannabis plants only for the propagation of those mature cannabis plants or for the production of cannabis seeds by those mature cannabis plants, but the area within a nursery cultivation facility in which mature cannabis plants are cultivated must be physically separated from the area within the facility in which immature cannabis plants and seedlings are cultivated. **A nursery cultivation facility may not sell, distribute or otherwise transfer to any person mature cannabis plants, cannabis flower or cannabis trim.**" The nursery is a *cuttings / immature-only* operation; it cannot sell flower.
- **§501(3)(D):** "A nursery cultivation facility may sell to consumers only immature cannabis plants, seedlings, cannabis seeds and agricultural or gardening supplies relating to the cultivation of cannabis." (The "to consumers" provision is the Maine-specific adult-use direct-to-consumer nursery license; the sales-tax and excise-tax applicability are spelled out in §501(3)(D)(2) and §501(9).)
- **§501(11):** "A tier 1 cultivation facility ..., a tier 2 cultivation facility ... and a nursery cultivation facility may sell to consumers through a delivery service operated under the same requirements as for cannabis stores under section 504, subsection 9, but a nursery cultivation facility may sell under this subsection only those items authorized for sale under subsection 3, paragraph D." **A nursery cultivation facility can also deliver clones and seeds to consumers — a delivery option for adult-use Maine growers who do not live near a physical nursery storefront.**

### What an adult-use 21+ Maine resident can do

Three legal paths to clones for an adult-use grower:

1. **Buy from an OCP-licensed Nursery Cultivation Facility** (28-B §501(3)(D) — direct consumer sales; §501(11) — delivery). Examples from the 5 vendors: Cannabis Seed Bank of Maine, Seed & Soil Maine, North Atlantic Seed Co. (seeds only).
2. **Buy from an OCP-licensed Cannabis Store** (per 28-B §501(2), cultivation facilities can sell to consumers *only if* they have a separate cannabis store license; cannabis stores are explicitly allowed to deliver "seeds, seedlings, immature cannabis plants" per the OCP FAQ at https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/resources/faq: "Yes. A cannabis store licensee may deliver seeds, seedlings, immature cannabis plants, cannabis, and cannabis products as well as other items sold by the cannabis store."). **A 21+ adult in Maine can have clones delivered to their door from any licensed cannabis store in the state** — this is the OCP's most grower-friendly recent rule and is worth highlighting in the blog.
3. **Become a registered medical patient** and then buy from a registered caregiver (Maine Clone Company, Maine Seedlings). A medical card requires a written certification from a Maine medical provider (physician, certified nurse practitioner, or physician assistant) per 22 MRSA §2423-B(2-C) and the OCP FAQ. The certification cost varies by provider; OCP does not charge for certifications.

### What an unregistered adult-use 21+ Maine resident CANNOT do

- Buy clones from Maine Clone Company or Maine Seedlings (medical-caregiver model; the vendor's own FAQ at maineclonecompany.com: "MED CARD AND PHOTO ID REQUIRED FOR PURCHASE"). The site does sell to visiting patients from approved states (per the OCP visiting-patients list: https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/medical-use/visiting-patients), but that's a medical reciprocity path, not an adult-use path.
- Buy from a registered caregiver *unless* they have a medical card. (The OCP FAQ confirms: "These restrictions do not apply to the cultivation of cannabis for medical use by a qualifying patient, a caregiver, a registered caregiver, or a registered dispensary as authorized by the Maine Medical Use of Cannabis Act." https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/resources/faq.)

### What an adult-use 21+ Maine resident CAN do without a medical card

- Grow up to **6 mature, 12 immature, unlimited seedlings** at home (per 28-B §1502, post-LD 555). Plant-tagged as "Personal Adult Use – Title 28-B, Section 1502" with name and ID number. Covered in the home grow guide.
- Buy seeds from any of the 5 Maine vendors (seeds are *legal* for 21+ adult-use buyers at any of the 5 vendors, regardless of med card status, because seeds are not a "cannabis product" under the 28-B statute — see §5 caveat).
- Buy clones from a Nursery Cultivation Facility or a cannabis store (and have them delivered).

## 8. The 7 beginner mistakes for clones

These are the most common, well-documented failure modes for a first-time clone buyer in Maine. Sources: Maine Clone Company FAQ, North Atlantic Seed's New England blog, Adkar-Purushothama 2023, the autoflower brief's transplant-shock discussion, and the home grow guide.

1. **"Buy the cheapest clone."** A $20 Cannabis Seed Bank of Maine small clone from a vendor with no documented HLVd testing protocol is a 10–20% loss-rate bet (transplant shock, HLVd, root rot). A $25 Maine Clone Company clone from a 14-year-old pheno-hunting operation with an HLVd primer on the website is the same price but a much better risk-adjusted buy. **Ask the vendor for the mother plant's testing protocol. If they can't tell you, walk away.**

2. **"Skip the quarantine period."** A clone goes from a 24-hour-T5 vendor cloner into a grower's outdoor garden or indoor tent on Day 1. If the clone has HLVd, russet mites, or fusarium, it spreads to the rest of the grow in days. **7-14 days in a separate space minimum** (per Maine Clone Company's primer: "Isolate any new cuttings or established plants coming from outside of your facility. Make sure to test these plants before integrating them with the rest of your grow"). The Adkar-Purushothama 2023 review is more conservative: 30-day quarantine with week-3 RT-PCR test.

3. **"Plant directly into the final container."** A 4"x4" rooted clone from Maine Clone Company should not go into a 25-gallon fabric pot on Day 1. The root system is small; the surrounding soil stays wet; pythium takes hold. **Pot up in 1-gallon → 3- or 5-gallon → final container** over 2-3 weeks. This is the same advice for any transplant, but the clone's vulnerability is higher than a seedling's.

4. **"Don't acclimate to outdoor sun."** The clone has been under T5 fluorescents at 24-hour light and ~70% humidity. Outdoor Maine sun in late May is full-spectrum UV at 14+ hours and ambient humidity 50-80%. **Harden off the clone for 3-7 days**: 1 hour outdoor morning sun on Day 1, 2 hours Day 2, 4 hours Day 3, etc. The classic "light burn" bleached-leaf symptom is the result of skipping this step.

5. **"Skip the root inspection."** When the clone arrives, turn the pot upside down and look at the root ball. **Healthy:** white, fibrous, no smell. **Unhealthy:** brown, mushy, sour smell (root rot); root-bound (mass of circling roots with no loose soil); bone-dry (the vendor under-watered). A 12" clone with a brown mushy root ball will not recover. Reject at pickup.

6. **"Ignore HLVd symptoms."** The most insidious clone failure. Per Adkar-Purushothama 2023: most HLVd-infected plants are **asymptomatic** until flower. If the clone has symptoms in veg — leaf bunching, shorter internodes, stunted growth compared to a sibling plant — the most likely cause is HLVd. **Destroy the plant; do not flower it; do not take cuttings from it.** The Maine Clone Co primer's "Symptoms" list (shorter internodal spacing, smaller leaves, stunting of bud size and morphology, reduction of trichomes, reduction of terpenes, reduced overall plant vigor) is the working diagnostic.

7. **"Plant too early."** Maine Clone Company's clones are 4-6 weeks old at pickup; they can be transplanted as soon as the last frost passes (May 20 in southern Maine, May 31 in central, June 1-14 in northern). The home grow guide's MOFGA transplant date (June 14) is the conservative date; May 15-31 is the median for southern/central. **The clone is already 4-6 weeks of veg old; the grower does not need to start indoor as early as for a seed.** Planting a clone in mid-April (when you'd start seeds) means an unnecessary 4-6 weeks of indoor grow cost.

## 9. Common Maine clone/seeds FAQ

### "Can I buy clones as an adult-use grower in Maine?"

Yes, but the path is specific. An adult-use 21+ Maine grower can buy clones from:
- An OCP-licensed Nursery Cultivation Facility (per 28-B §501(3)(D)) — Cannabis Seed Bank of Maine and Seed & Soil Maine fit this pattern.
- An OCP-licensed Cannabis Store (per OCP FAQ: "A cannabis store licensee may deliver seeds, seedlings, immature cannabis plants, cannabis, and cannabis products").
- A registered caregiver, **only if** the grower is also a registered medical patient (per 22 MRSA §2423-A(2)(K)).

Maine Clone Company and Maine Seedlings are caregiver-only; an unregistered adult-use grower cannot buy clones from them.

### "Are clones better than seeds for Maine's short season?"

**For photoperiods, yes.** A clone saves 3-5 weeks of calendar time, which is the difference between a finished harvest and a frosted one in central / northern Maine. **For autoflowers, no** — the autoflowering trait is age-driven, not light-cycle-driven, so a clone adds no advantage (and the industry doesn't sell them; Maine Clone Company: "Do you sell autoflowering clones? No, unfortunately it's impossible to keep an autoflowering mother as it would flower and die even under 24 hours of light").

### "How do I know if a clone is healthy?"

At the vendor (in person, mandatory for the 5 Maine vendors — none ship clones):
- **Roots:** white, fibrous, no smell. Reject: brown, mushy, sour.
- **Stems:** no discoloration (red/purple is OK on some strains; black or cankered is not), no mushy spots, no white powdery mildew.
- **Leaves:** no spots, no webbing (spider mites), no leaf-curling (russet mites), no chlorotic mosaic pattern (viral infection), no powdery mildew.
- **Top growth:** actively growing tip, no yellowing new leaves (nutrient deficiency or root issue), no "dudding" pattern (shorter internodes, leaf bunching — HLVd).

At home, quarantine 7-14 days in a separate space. Re-inspect at Day 7. Test by RT-PCR if available.

### "Can I ship clones to Maine from out of state?"

Maine has no specific ban on inter-state clone shipping, but **most clone vendors do not ship across state lines** for legal/liability reasons. Maine Clone Company's FAQ is explicit: "For logistical and legal reasons, we do not ship clones." Maine Seedlings is pickup-only. Cannabis Seed Bank of Maine is walk-in / phone order. The inter-state clone market is essentially nonexistent in 2026.

**Stick to Maine vendors for clone pickup.** If a grower is buying seeds, inter-state shipping is the standard model (ILGM, MSNL, North Atlantic Seed, etc.).

### "What is HLVd and how do I avoid it?"

HLVd is a 256-nucleotide circular RNA viroid that infects cannabis, causing "dudding disease" — reduced vigor, smaller buds, reduced trichomes, reduced terpenes, 30-50% yield loss in susceptible cultivars (Adkar-Purushothama 2023, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10053334/). It spreads via mechanical transmission (dirty tools, gloves, water) and is asymptomatic in most cannabis cultivars until flower.

To avoid:
- **Buy clones from a vendor with a documented HLVd testing protocol.** Ask: "Do you test mother plants by RT-PCR / qPCR?" If yes, on what schedule?
- **Quarantine new clones for 7-30 days** before integrating with the main grow (Adkar-Purushothama 2023: 30 days; Maine Clone Co primer: 7-14 days is the practical minimum).
- **Use new gloves per plant and sterilize cutting tools in 10% bleach for 10 seconds between plants** (per the Adkar-Purushothama 2023 review). Isopropyl alcohol does **not** kill HLVd.
- **If you suspect HLVd, destroy the plant.** Do not flower it. Do not take cuttings from it. Tissue culture is the only known cure (meristem tip culture + cold treatment), and it is expensive.

### "Can I take a clone from a friend's plant?"

Legally: depends on whether you are both in the same caregiver network (per 22 MRSA §2423-A(2)(K) and (K-1)). Practically: high pest risk without testing. Not recommended for first-year growers. **The blog should advise: don't take friend-clones unless you have the testing infrastructure to verify HLVd status, or unless the friend is a registered caregiver you trust and you are a registered patient.**

### "Are autoflower clones available?"

**No, and not worth it.** Maine Clone Company: "No, unfortunately it's impossible to keep an autoflowering mother as it would flower and die even under 24 hours of light." The autoflowering trait is incompatible with the clone model. The autoflower-vs-feminized brief is the source of truth on autoflower seed selection.

## 10. The decision matrix

### Choose CLONES if...

- **You're growing photoperiods.** (Autoflowers don't benefit from clones; the trait is age-driven.)
- **You're in northern or central Maine with a tight season.** (The 3-5 week head start is the difference between finishing and not finishing.)
- **You want a known phenotype.** (A clone of a pheno-hunted mother gives you certainty; a seed gives you a lottery ticket.)
- **You can verify the vendor's HLVd testing protocol.** (Ask the question; accept nothing less than a clear "yes, RT-PCR, every mother, quarterly" or equivalent.)
- **You're willing to quarantine the clone for 7-14 days** (or 30 days with an RT-PCR test, per Adkar-Purushothama 2023).
- **You can drive to a Maine vendor** (Farmington, Waterville, Biddeford / Scarborough, Monroe).

### Choose SEEDS if...

- **You want a specific strain not at your local vendor.** (Seed catalog depth is the seed path's moat.)
- **You don't want pest risk.** (A seed carries no HLVd, no russet mites, no fusarium. Clean start, clean plant.)
- **You want to start cheap.** (ILGM 10-pack at $99 = $9.90/seed. Hard to beat on cost.)
- **You're growing autoflowers.** (Autoflower clones don't exist; autoflower seeds are the only path.)
- **You live outside southern/central Maine and can't drive to a clone vendor.** (Online seed banks ship.)
- **You want to grow from seed to harvest in a single indoor tent** without ever leaving the controlled environment.

### Choose BOTH (insurance) if...

- **You're a serious Maine grower with the budget for one pack of each.** A clone of the strain you really want, AND a backup seed pack of the same strain (or a different strain with similar flower time). If the clone fails, the seed takes its place. If the seed germinates weakly, the clone is the main plant. The $30-40 backup-seed insurance is the cheapest yield-protection policy in the garden.
- **You're in central or northern Maine and growing a 7-week photoperiod.** The clone is the main plant (proven genetics, head start). The seed is the backup (in case the clone fails HLVd testing or transplant).
- **You're a beginner doing your first grow.** A clone + a backup seed = learning the clone cycle AND having a fallback. Two grows in one season.
