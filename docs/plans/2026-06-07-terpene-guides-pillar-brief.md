# Terpene Guides Pillar — Research Brief

Status: brief only, not a content production plan. Goal: capture intent, source-list, and structure so articles can be written during the affiliate approval wait window (next 1-2 weeks) without rediscovering the research.

Caveat: terpene pharmacology is an active research area. Several commonly-cited claims (myrcene "couch-lock" at 0.5%, mango-myrcene enhancing THC, "indica vs sativa" predicting effect) are folk-knowledge, not settled science. Articles in this pillar must cite primary literature and flag folk claims explicitly — anything else fails E-E-A-T and risks YMYL penalties in Google's helpful-content system.

---

## Why this pillar (rationale)

1. **Maine-specific moat**. Maine's 18-691 CMR Ch. 40 testing rule mandates cannabinoid profiling and 7 other analyte categories but does NOT mandate terpene testing. Only some Maine testing facilities offer terpene panels, and only some cultivators pay for them. This is a real knowledge gap for Maine consumers trying to read dispensary menus.
2. **Adjacent to existing pillars**. The P1 drying page already mentions "terpene preservation" once; P2 indoor setup mentions "terpene profile" once; the existing autoflower vs feminized page cross-references terpene effects implicitly. A dedicated terpene pillar connects to all four existing cluster pages.
3. **No new regulatory complexity**. Terpene content is informational, not regulatory. No OCP, no caregiver-vs-nursery split (Pitfall 39), no advertising compliance beyond the FTC disclosure on any product links (none planned).
4. **Filler for the affiliate wait window**. 1-2 weeks is enough to write 2-3 cornerstone articles if the research is already done.

## Source list (verified 2026-06-07, primary sources preferred)

### Primary literature (citation-ready, peer-reviewed)

| Compound | Citation | Key finding | Maine relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| β-Caryophyllene | Gertsch et al. 2008 (PNAS) — "Beta-caryophyllene is a dietary cannabinoid" | First identification of a terpene as a CB2-selective agonist; binds at ~155 nM | Mechanism claim is solid; "dietary cannabinoid" framing is publishable |
| Myrcene | LaVigne et al. 2021 — opioid receptor modulation in rodent models | Preclinical evidence for myrcene's sedating mechanism; human-equivalent dose unverified | Cite as "preclinical only" — common folk claim is pharmacologically indefensible at cannabis-consumption concentrations |
| Linalool | Harada et al. 2018 (Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience) | GABAergic anxiolytic mechanism in mice; same pathway as benzodiazepines | Strongest mechanistic claim of any terpene; cite with confidence |
| Limonene | Song et al. 2021 — adenosine A2A-mediated effects; 2024 Johns Hopkins clinical trial | 30mg THC + 15mg d-limonene reduced anxiety vs. THC alone in humans | First clinical validation of the entourage effect; cite prominently |
| α-Pinene | Russo 2011 (British Journal of Pharmacology, "Taming THC") | AChE inhibition hypothesis for counteracting THC-induced memory impairment | Hypothesis only, untested in humans at cannabis-consumption levels |
| Volatility | Multiple, summarized in cannascience.org volatility section | ~31% terpene loss in 1 week, ~55% at 3 months; monoterpenes degrade fastest | Directly relevant to the P1 drying page's "fresh vs. cured" framing |
| Maine regulatory | 18-691 CMR Ch. 40 (verified 2026-06-07, OCP open-data + Adult Use Annual Report 2025) | 8 mandatory analyte categories; terpene panel NOT in the mandatory list; 17,701 samples tested in 2025, 6.4% fail rate | The Maine-specific angle: terpene data is supplementary, not regulatory |

### Secondary sources (use for context, not for primary claims)

- cannanscience.org/terpenes/profiles — pharmacology summary with named researchers, binding affinities, honest assessment of what evidence does and does not support. **Best secondary source for editorial tone.**
- phenodb.eu/terpenes — strain × terpene database, useful for "if you want X terpene, look for Y strain" examples. Strain counts and average percentages are useful but strain genetics are international, not Maine-specific.
- tribetokes.com/learn/cannabis-education/terpenes — six-terpene reference, good for the user-experience layer (which strains, what users say).
- goodrx.com/health-topic/cannabis/terpene-guide — consumer-facing, useful as a structural reference for the audience-level article (not for primary claims).
- herb.co/guides/types-cannabis-terpenes-flavors — has the boiling-point / vaporization temperature table (myrcene 334°F, limonene 349°F, etc.) — useful for the consumption-methods article.
- maine.gov/dafs/ocp/open-data/adult-use/testing-data — Maine OCP testing data portal. Quarterly updates. Could be cited as evidence that terpene panels are not in the mandatory Maine data set.

### Source-list anti-patterns to avoid

- Strain database sites with affiliate-link strain seeders (Leafly, Allbud, Wikileaf) — they have the data but the editorial model is incompatible with Pitfall 38 (affiliate scope discipline).
- "Indica vs. sativa" claims that don't acknowledge the modern research consensus (the categories are folk taxonomy; effect is driven by chemistry, not plant morphology).
- "Mango before smoking" myth and similar folk-knowledge claims — cite the LaVigne 2021 dose-response argument that refutes them.

## Article 1 outline (cornerstone)

**Working title**: Cannabis terpenes explained: a Maine consumer's guide to the six most common

**Sections**:
1. **What a terpene is** (200 words) — definition, role in plants generally, why cannabis gets the spotlight
2. **The six most common cannabis terpenes** (table) — myrcene, β-caryophyllene, limonene, α-pinene, linalool, terpinolene. For each: aroma, non-cannabis sources, what the research says about effects, what users say, what strains are typically high in it
3. **The entourage effect, with citations** (400 words) — Russo 2011 framing, LaVigne 2021, 2024 Johns Hopkins limonene trial. Be explicit: "the entourage effect is real but the clinical evidence is in early stages"
4. **Why "indica vs sativa" doesn't predict effect** (300 words) — the modern research consensus, with citations. This is the contrarian hook that will rank for "indica sativa myth" searches
5. **What this means for Maine consumers** (300 words) — most Maine dispensary menus do not publish terpene panels; the cultivator pays extra for terpene testing (it's not in Maine's 18-691 CMR Ch. 40 mandatory panel); budtenders at the better stores can read you the COA; ask for it
6. **Maine-specific callout: how to ask for terpene data at your dispensary** (200 words) — what to ask, what to expect, what to do with the answer
7. **FAQ + further reading** (300 words)

## Article 2 outline

**Working title**: How to read a Maine cannabis COA for terpenes (and why most menus don't have them)

**Sections**:
1. **What a COA is** — Certificate of Analysis, the document every batch gets from a Maine-certified testing facility
2. **Maine's mandatory testing panel** (18-691 CMR Ch. 40) — the 8 categories, listed explicitly. Note: terpenes are NOT in the mandatory list
3. **What the COA contains** — sample, batch ID, test date, methodology, all 8 panels, pass/fail status
4. **Where terpene data shows up** — only if (a) the cultivator paid for terpene analysis (most don't) and (b) the testing facility offers terpene panels (most do, but it's not free)
5. **How to read terpene numbers** — total terpene % by weight, dominant vs. trace, the "above 0.5% matters" rule of thumb, the volatility caveat
6. **Maine OCP's open testing data** — maine.gov/dafs/ocp/open-data/adult-use/testing-data, what it does and doesn't include
7. **Sample walkthrough** — anonymized COA, point at each section
8. **FAQ**

**Length**: 1,200-1,500 words. This is the Maine-specific differentiation article — no other Maine site has it.

## Article 3 outline

**Working title**: Terpene preservation during drying and curing: what the science says vs. what the internet says

**Sections**:
1. **The volatility problem** — 31% loss in 1 week, 55% in 3 months; monoterpenes degrade faster than sesquiterpenes
2. **What "Internet wisdom" gets wrong** — the 60/60 drying rule is good for mold prevention but is not optimized for terpene retention; high-temperature drying kills terpenes
3. **What the science says about drying temperatures** — vaporization points per compound (myrcene 334°F, limonene 349°F, linalool 388°F), the implication for drying
4. **Curing science** — what the literature actually says (limited; mostly anecdotal) vs. what the controlled studies show
5. **Storage** — temperature, light, container; the practical implications
6. **Maine-specific angle** — October 84-86% humidity (already established on P1 drying page) interacts with the terpene-loss curve; the practical protocol

**Length**: 1,500-1,800 words. Connects directly to the P1 drying page; both reinforce each other.

## Article 4 outline

**Working title**: Buying cannabis by effect, not by strain name: a terpene-based selection framework

**Sections**:
1. **Why strain names are unreliable** — same name, different chemistry, different batches; "Blue Dream" from one cultivator ≠ "Blue Dream" from another

## Risk callouts

- **Medical claims risk**: the limonene-anxiety, linalool-GABA, caryophyllene-anti-inflammatory effects are real pharmacology but are NOT FDA-approved medical claims. Articles must use "preclinical evidence" / "user reports" / "in rodent models" framing consistently. Do not say "limonene treats anxiety"; say "preclinical evidence suggests limonene modulates serotonin and dopamine pathways; a 2024 clinical trial found reduced anxiety when 15mg d-limonene was combined with 30mg THC vs. THC alone". The distinction is the kind of thing that gets YMYL pages demoted.
- **"Indica vs sativa" debunking is politically sensitive**: the existing P0 home-grow pillar uses the categories as a Maine-cannabis-shopping heuristic. Articles 1 and 4 should not delete the categories but reframe them: "indica and sativa describe plant morphology and growth habit; the chemical profile (cannabinoids + terpenes) is what predicts effect".
- **Strain database claims are not Maine-applicable**: when citing "high-myrcene strains" examples, the percentages are from international databases; Maine-grown flower from a different cultivator may differ. Always frame as "examples include", never "this strain is X% myrcene at Maine dispensaries".
