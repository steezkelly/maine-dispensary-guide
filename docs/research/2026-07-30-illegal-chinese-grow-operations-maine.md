## Editorial guardrails

- Use the user's requested phrase in the title/route, but explain that it is a shorthand for a documented subset of cases, not a claim about every illegal grow or every Chinese person in Maine.
- Distinguish: (1) convictions/guilty pleas and official findings, (2) indictment/charging-document allegations, (3) investigative reporting, (4) sworn or recorded worker accounts, and (5) policy testimony/opinion.
- Do not state that the Chinese government directed Maine grows; the congressional testimony and national reports do not prove that Maine-specific proposition.
- Do not state that all workers were trafficked. Maine's 2024 U.S. Attorney statement said investigators had not found evidence of human trafficking at that time; later worker accounts and reporting describe allegations that warrant investigation but do not themselves establish the crime.
- Do not infer criminality from a surname, nationality, immigration status, or a property address. The 2024 USAO-ME statement expressly warned against that inference.
- Do not publish exact residential addresses unless they appear in an official release and are necessary to understand a concluded public case. Prefer town/county names.
- Use the phrase `Chinese nationals`, `Chinese-linked`, or `alleged Chinese-run network` only when tied to a named source and case. Never use `Chinese` as a substitute for `criminal`.

## Source quality map

### Tier 1: primary government/legal records

- Maine statute pages: legal authorization and criminal boundary.
- USAO-ME releases: dated enforcement snapshots, guilty pleas, sentence, agency statements.
- USAO-MA charging release: allegations and presumption-of-innocence language.
- Maine State Police release: one detailed physical-search case.
- Maine AG written testimony: state-law/enforcement framing.
- OCP open-data portal: current roster availability and limitations.
- Congressional hearing testimony: evidence of what witnesses asserted, not independent proof of every assertion.

### Tier 2: local investigative reporting

- Portland Press Herald / Maine Sunday Telegram: enforcement counts, local sheriff quotations, court-document worker accounts, and a reported Turner licensing network.
- News Center Maine: later reporting on court documents and a China, Maine worker call; source was discoverable but access-denied to the extractor on 2026-07-30, so it is not the sole support for any claim in the article.

### Excluded or limited

- Maine Wire and partisan advocacy pages: useful leads, not used as sole support for material claims.
- Search-result snippets: discovery only; not cited as evidence.
- Social posts and anonymous allegations without an official record: excluded.

## Outline

1. Answer-first: the public record supports a documented illegal-grow problem and a case-specific Chinese-linked subset, not a universal ethnic or geopolitical conclusion.
2. What is documented: legal boundary, 2024 enforcement snapshot, detailed Parsonsfield and 2026 federal cases, 2026 enforcement slowdown reporting.
3. Where the Chinese-linked evidence is strongest: the July 2025 federal charging release and the concluded Guo case; keep allegations and convictions separate.
4. Worker accounts and testimony: worker call, law-enforcement response, USAO-ME statement, AG testimony, sheriff testimony, OCP tracking testimony.
5. What the public record cannot currently answer: exact count, state direction, trafficking prevalence, current licensing overlap, and who ultimately controls each property.
6. Resident/consumer response: do not confront or enter; call 911 for immediate danger, local law enforcement for a suspected crime, and use official OCP channels for licensing/regulatory questions.
7. Source matrix and method note.
