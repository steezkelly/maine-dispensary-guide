## Regulatory vocabulary (four-state)

Hemp in Maine spans multiple current / enacted / failed / federal states. Do not collapse them.

1. **Operative Maine statute:** Title 7 M.R.S. §2231 (Chapter 406-A, Hemp), as amended by PL 2025, c. 416.
2. **Operative agency rules:** DACF rules for growing hemp (Chapter 274); DACF operates Maine's USDA-approved hemp production plan.
3. **Enacted and operative June 2025:** PL 2025, c. 416 (LD 1920), emergency law taking effect June 24/25, 2025, adding the "potentially intoxicating hemp product" category and 21+ / packaging restrictions.
4. **FAILED / dead:** LD 1983 (governor's bill to regulate intoxicating hemp under adult-use cannabis) — died Jan 13, 2026 (Leave to Withdraw). It is NOT a pending law; label as dead.
5. **Federal (volatile, contested):** FY2026 continuing-appropriations provision (signed Nov 12, 2025) redefining hemp to a total-THC standard with a delayed effective date (~Nov 2026), contested by H.R. 7024 / H.R. 7010 / Senate delay bills. Present as federal watch, not operative Maine law.

## Source ledger

### S1 — DACF Hemp Licensing (primary authoritative program page)
URL: https://www.maine.gov/dacf/php/hemp (updated Aug 3, 2026)
Claims:
- DACF is the licensing authority for growing hemp in Maine. [safest: "The Maine Department of Agriculture, Conservation and Forestry (DACF) licenses the growing of hemp in Maine."]
- Hemp is low-THC Cannabis sativa grown for CBD, fiber, food, and more; legal to grow in the US but regulated; a license is required. [safest: "In Maine you need a DACF license to grow hemp."]
- All hemp licensed in Maine meets USDA's definition of hemp: Total THC (dry weight) at or below 0.3%, and can be marketed nationwide. [safest: "DACF-licensed hemp must test at or below 0.3% Total THC on a dry-weight basis."]
- DACF does not license or regulate high-THC cannabis (marijuana). [safest: "DACF does not license high-THC cannabis; that is the Office of Cannabis Policy's role."]
- DACF does not regulate the processing of hemp and keeps no list of processors or markets. [safest: "DACF does not license or regulate hemp processing."]
- License process: apply at least 30 days before planting; $100 nonrefundable application fee; a signed license agreement is needed before planting. [safest: "Apply at least 30 days before you plant, with the $100 non-refundable application fee."]
- Steps: application review → draft agreement → sign/mail with fees → register grow sites at USDA-FSA → plant in discreet lots by variety → planting report within 14 days → inspection → keep program informed → 30-day pre-harvest notice if unsampled → sampling → post-harvest report. [safest: "DACF publishes an 11-step grower process summarized on its hemp page."]
- Hemp cannot exceed 0.3% Total THC by dry weight; higher-THC crops are destroyed if remediation is not feasible. [safest: "Hemp above 0.3% Total THC on a dry-weight basis must be destroyed if remediation is not feasible."]
- Fees: $100 application; $100 license for seedlings/immature leafy greens/microgreens; $100 for research with an educational institution; $250 for seed-for-food-oil/grain, fiber, immature plants for food/feed, breeding <100 sq ft, soil remediation; $500 + $50/acre outdoor floral/viable seed/other commercial; $500 + $0.25/sq ft indoor floral/viable seed/other commercial; max license fee $20,000; $60 per additional lot beyond the one included THC analysis. [safest: "DACF's published fee schedule ranges from a $100 application fee to a $500 license fee plus per-acre or per-square-foot charges, capped at $20,000."]
- Maine does not certify hemp seed and publishes no list of approved/prohibited strains. [safest: "Maine does not certify hemp seed and publishes no list of approved cultivars."]
- Grower must retain third-party THC test documentation (COA) for purchased seed/seedlings/clones, submitted with the planting report. [safest: "You must keep third-party lab documentation of parent THC content for the seed or clones you plant."]
- Planting report due within 14 days after planting; must confirm planting location and that seed stock was ≤0.3% Total THC. [safest: "A planting report is due within 14 days after planting."]
- Crops testing above the limit are destroyed in a manner approved by the Department; licensee pays all destruction costs. [safest: "Over-limit crops are destroyed in a DACF-approved manner at the grower's expense."]
- Growers licensed 2026 list available (updated 8/3/2026). [optional]
- Contact: Hemp Program, 28 State House Station, Augusta ME 04333; State Horticulturist Gary Fish (207) 287-7545.

### S2 — Title 7 M.R.S. §2231 (Chapter 406-A, Hemp) — codified statute
URL: https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/statutes/7/title7sec2231.html (extraction page note dated 10/20/2025)
Claims:
- Definition of hemp: "the plant Cannabis sativa L. and any part of that plant, including the seeds and all derivatives... with a delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol concentration of not more than 0.3% on a dry weight basis, or as otherwise defined in federal law." Hemp does not include medical cannabis (Title 22 c.558-C) or adult-use cannabis (Title 28-B c.1). [safest: quote/paraphrase with the 0.3% delta-9 dry-weight definition and the exclusion of medical/adult-use cannabis.]
- Growing permitted only with a grower license issued under subsection 4. [safest: "A person may plant, grow, harvest, possess, process, sell and buy hemp only with a grower license."]
- License is valid for one year and only for the site(s) specified. [safest: "The license is valid for one year for the site(s) listed."]
- Grower must plant only from certified seed source, saved seed (with 3rd-party testing), clones from certified-source seeds, or tissue cultures from certified-source live plants. [safest: paraphrase.]
- Within 14 days of planting, grower must list varieties and state seeds/clones meet the hemp definition. [safest: "A variety listing and hemp-definition statement are due within 14 days of planting."]
- Fees ranges set by statute: application ≥$50 and ≤$100; license ≥$100 and ≤$500; monitoring/sampling/testing ≥$1 and ≤$100 per acre, ≤25¢ per sq ft indoor. [safest: "The statute sets fee ranges that DACF implements by rule."]
- Subsection 10: Hemp and hemp-derived products are NOT tracked as part of medical (Title 22 c.558-C) or adult-use (Title 28-B c.1) cannabis programs. [safest: "Maine law says hemp is not tracked as medical or adult-use cannabis."]
- Subsection 11: annual report to the Legislature each April 1 covering license count, acreage/square footage, harvested pounds, and product types. [safe: "DACF files an annual hemp report each April 1."]
- Subsection 12 (PL 2025 c.416): a potentially intoxicating hemp product may not be sold to anyone under 21. [safest: "Sales of potentially intoxicating hemp products to anyone under 21 are prohibited."]
- Subsection 13 (PL 2025 c.416): packaging/labeling — potentially intoxicating hemp products that are not beverages, salves, or topicals must be in child-resistant and tamper-evident packaging; no labeling that violates federal trademark rules or causes reasonable consumer confusion. [safest: quote/paraphrase.]
- Definitions added by PL 2025 c.416: F "nonintoxicating cannabinoid" (CBD, THCV, CBC, CBTC, CBL, CBE, CBG, CBDV, CBN); G "potentially intoxicating cannabinoids" (list incl. delta-8, delta-9, delta-10, delta-7 THCs and isomers, HHC, THCa-related, etc.); H "potentially intoxicating hemp product" = hemp-derived products that in final form do NOT contain (<0.3% potentially intoxicating cannabinoids AND >10:1 nonintoxicating-to-intoxicating ratio), i.e. a product IS a potentially intoxicating hemp product when it has 0.3%+ potentially intoxicating cannabinoids OR a ≤10:1 ratio. [safest: carefully paraphrase; avoid engineering the ratio language beyond the statute.]

### S3 — PL 2025, c. 416 (LD 1920) — emergency chapter law, effective June 2025
URL: https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/display_ps.asp?LD=1920&snum=132
Claims:
- Title: "An Act to Prohibit the Sale of Potentially Intoxicating Hemp Products to a Person Under 21 Years of Age."
- Emergency; final disposition "Emergency Enacted, Jun 24, 2025" with "Governor's Action: Emergency Unsigned, Jun 24, 2025." [safest: "The bill became law as an unsigned emergency measure, effective June 24, 2025."]
- Amends Title 7 §2231 (definitions, subsections 12 and 13) and also Title 28-A (liquor) sections 2 (definitions), 83-B, 1231, 1652. [safest: "The law also touched liquor-code definitions and registration provisions."]
- Creates "potentially intoxicating hemp product" category.

### S7 — DACF Criminal History Guidance (PDF, dated Jan 3, 2025)
URL: https://www.maine.gov/dacf/php/hemp/documents/2025-criminal-history-guidance.pdf
Claims:
- USDA requires anyone applying to grow hemp to request an FBI criminal history report and submit it to the licensing authority. [safest: as stated.]
- Report must go back 10 years, be dated within 60 days of applying, cover state + federal history; fee $18. [safest: as stated.]
- Fingerprinting via Idemia/IdentoGO Print-n-Go (Augusta, Waterville, Auburn, Bangor) or USPS. [safest: "options are limited to IdentoGO Print-n-Go sites currently in Augusta, Waterville, Auburn, and Bangor, or USPS locations."]
- Person with state/federal felony conviction relating to a controlled substance may not grow hemp for 10 years from conviction; exemption for 2014 Farm Bill §7606 producers before Dec 20, 2018 whose conviction was before that date. [safest: as stated.]
- FBI sends results electronically; forward PDF to hemp program. [safest: as stated.]

### S8 — DACF FSA Reporting Guidance (PDF)
URL: https://www.maine.gov/dacf/php/hemp/documents/FSA_Reporting_2024.pdf
Claims:
- USDA requires all hemp production reported to Farm Service Agency (FSA), in addition to DACF planting report; DACF cannot transfer for you. [safest: as stated.]
- Report to FSA when planting complete; add FSA lot codes to DACF planting report (due within 2 weeks) or crop cannot be sampled/tested. [safest: as stated.]
- Form FSA-578 Report of Acreage; report by hemp lots; identify on FSA maps location/acreage. [safest: as stated.]
- Maine license number prefixed "23_" (e.g., "1550" → "23_1550") to USDA/FSA. [safest: as stated.]
- Keep FSA-578 copy at least 3 years. [safest: as stated.]
- Provide: final license agreement, landowner agreement, incorporation docs, producer identity/EIN or SSN, field addresses/coordinates, lot planted date, variety, intended use. [safest: as stated.]

### S9 — Maine State Hemp Program Annual Report FY2024 (submitted December 2025)
URL: https://legislature.maine.gov/doc/12503
Claims (use as dated program context, not a current live count):
- 2024 = ninth year; received 14 applications, issued 12 final license agreements on 32 acres; ~5.75 acres actually planted; one indoor license for 100 sq ft never planted. [safest: "DACF's FY2024 annual report recorded 12 final license agreements across about 32 acres, with about 5.75 acres planted."]
- Market relatively flat; most successful model = vertically integrated smaller floral hemp growers making cannabinoid products for niche markets. [safest: as stated, as report characterization.]
- Hemp fiber and grain markets have not materialized for lack of processing infrastructure; single grain grower in recent seasons. [safest: as stated, as report characterization.]
- Program requires roughly 150-200 growers to be fully self-funding; may consider turning licensing over to USDA if growers fall below 50. [safest: as stated, as report's own assessment.]
- FY2026 federal appropriations (Nov 2025) reworks hemp definition, effectively banning intoxicating hemp in consumer products; may also affect full-spectrum tinctures/topicals core to Maine growers. [safest: as stated, as report observation.]
- Top in-state products from survey: CBD/CBG extracts, tinctures, full-spectrum oil, gummies, smokable bud, spliffs, topicals, biomass, pet treats/supplements, beverages, capsules. [optional; light use.]
- ACF-Committee-requested stakeholder group recommended age-gating all hemp products (other than fiber and grain) to 21+; recommendation only, not enacted law. [safest: "a stakeholder group recommended age-gating; that is a recommendation, not law."]
