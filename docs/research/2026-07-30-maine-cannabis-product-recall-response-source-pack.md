# Maine Cannabis Product Recall and Failed-Test Response — Source Pack

- Collected: 2026-07-30
- Scope: Maine adult-use cannabis operators; this is an operational orientation page, not legal, medical, or regulatory advice.
- Approved route: `/guides/maine-cannabis-product-recall-response`
- Volatility: recall listings, testing data, rules, and OCP instructions can change; the page uses dated source notes and directs operators to the current OCP notice.

## Source hierarchy

### 1. Maine Office of Cannabis Policy: recalls and advisories

- URL: https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/resources/recalls
- Accessed: 2026-07-30
- Use: OCP's index of medical patient advisories and adult-use recalls. It identifies recall/advisory type, primary licensee/registrant, product type, and strain/flavor. It is the starting point for checking whether OCP has posted an incident.
- Limitation: the page is a live index; an operator must open the individual notice and follow the notice-specific product, batch, location, and action instructions.

### 2. OCP Green Trap adult-use recall notice

- URL: https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/news-events/news/maine-office-cannabis-policy-issues-health-and-safety-recall-adult-use-flower-0
- Accessed: 2026-07-30
- Use: dated example of a health-and-safety recall. OCP identifies the affected producer, strain, product sizes, batch numbers, sale window, and seven retail locations; it tells consumers to return or dispose of affected product and directs people with symptoms or adverse reactions to a healthcare provider or 911 for a medical emergency.
- Safe editorial use: present this as an example of how a notice identifies scope and consumer action, not as a universal response template or medical statement for every incident.

### 3. OCP Adult Use Testing Data

- URL: https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/open-data/adult-use/testing-data
- Accessed: 2026-07-30
- Page status: last updated July 15, 2026, according to the page.
- Use: OCP says adult-use cannabis and products must pass mandatory testing before sale; listed analyte categories include residual solvents, harmful chemicals/metals, yeast/mold and mycotoxins, harmful microbes, pesticides/fungicides/insecticides, and potency/homogeneity/cannabinoid profiles. OCP says mandatory results are reported to the licensee and OCP within two business days through a COA and electronic data deliverables.
- Critical limitation: OCP says the public tables represent initial mandatory testing only and do not include non-mandatory research/development or retest samples. Do not use the public failure table as a complete incident history for a business.
- Disposition boundary: OCP states that retesting is available in limited circumstances; it describes remediation as permitted for some categories and not permitted for pesticides and metals, where two retest samples may be submitted after an initial failure. The page points to the operative Adult Use Program rules for details.
- Data snapshot: Q2 2026 page table reports 207 individual sample fails out of 4,490 samples and a 4.61% fail rate, with the explicit caveat that retest data are not included. Use only if the page needs a dated context note; do not frame it as a business risk prediction.

### 4. Maine Title 28-B §105: tracking and recall traceability

- URL: https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/28-B/title28-Bsec105.html
- Accessed: 2026-07-30
- Use: the tracking system must maintain a detailed record from immature plant through retail sale, return, disposal, or destruction. The statute requires rules to include record-keeping necessary for the office's ability to implement a health-and-safety recall.
- Editorial consequence: the operator checklist should tell readers to preserve and reconcile batch identifiers, manifests, point-of-sale/inventory records, return records, disposal/destruction records, and distribution locations. It should not claim a specific software screen or reporting deadline unless an OCP source supports it.

### 5. Maine Title 28-B §803-A: administrative holds

- URL: https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/28-B/title28-Bsec803-A.html
- Accessed: 2026-07-30
- Use: OCP may impose an administrative hold when it has reasonable grounds related to, among other things, labeling/packaging, testing results, contamination, or tracking compliance. The notice must state the basis, identify affected cannabis/plants/products, describe operational restrictions, and indicate required actions.
- Operational claims supported: during a hold, affected items must be physically segregated; the licensee may not sell, give away, transfer, transport, dispose of, or destroy held items. The statute says OCP may terminate a hold, and a hold may not exceed 30 consecutive days from notice under this section.
- Editorial boundary: distinguish an operator's voluntary stop-sale/quarantine decision from a statutory OCP administrative hold. Do not tell readers that every failed test automatically creates a §803-A hold.

### 6. Adult-use testing rule materials

- Current-source landing page: https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/adult-use/rules-statutes
- Current OCP testing data page: https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/open-data/adult-use/testing-data
- Proposed rule watch (not operative): https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/sites/maine.gov.dafs.ocp/files/inline-files/18-691%20CMR%20Ch.%2040%20for%20Public%20Comment%202026.pdf
- Accessed: 2026-07-30
- Use: link operators to the current OCP rules/statutes landing page rather than reproducing a rule subsection whose status could change. Any mention of the 2026 Chapter 40 proposal must be labeled proposed, subject to review, and not effective.
- Do not use proposed-rule language as current instructions. The page should state the high-level OCP testing-data distinction—limited retesting, remediation where permitted, and destruction where required by the applicable current rule or OCP order—and tell operators to check the current rule and their testing facility/OCP instructions.

### 7. OCP Adult Use Compliance Data

- URL: https://www.maine.gov/dafs/ocp/open-data/adult-use/compliance-data
- Accessed: 2026-07-30
- Use: OCP describes minor, major, and major public-safety license violations; possible penalties include monetary penalties, suspension, or revocation. The page explains that its table is final-action data, includes redacted documents, and may differ from annual reports.
- Editorial consequence: keep compliance history, notices, settlement agreements, and corrective actions in the post-incident record; do not promise that a voluntary response prevents enforcement or state a fine amount without a case-specific source.

## Prohibited or softened claims

- No claim that every failed batch must immediately be destroyed.
- No claim that every failed test automatically triggers an OCP administrative hold or recall.
- No claim that a voluntary quarantine satisfies every legal duty.
- No medical diagnosis, treatment, or symptom interpretation beyond quoting OCP's incident-specific consumer direction.
- No universal recall deadline, response window, or fine amount without a specific current notice/order.
- No guarantee that a documented response prevents penalties, license action, litigation, or customer harm.
- No claim that the Q2 2026 fail rate predicts a particular operator's risk.
- No comparison of adult-use recalls with medical patient advisories as if they were the same event.
